# Church Growth Reporting Framework

This framework is meant to shape executive reports for a multi-campus church. The goal is to move the dashboard from "what happened" into "what is changing, why it may be changing, and what leadership should do next."

## Reporting Posture

Strong reports should compare each campus primarily against its own history, maturity stage, seasonality, and known events. Cross-campus comparisons are useful for learning, but a larger campus should not automatically be treated as healthier than a smaller campus.

Use three lenses in every report:

- Reach: attendance, first-time guests, online reach if available, event lift, and year-over-year momentum.
- Connection: kids, groups, Growth Track, volunteer engagement, and returning guest conversion.
- Capacity: volunteer coverage, volunteer leader pipeline, staff transitions, campus pastor changes, and service-time load.

## Benchmarks To Encode

These are starting ranges, not final judgments. A report should flag variance, then explain whether the variance is healthy, seasonal, data-quality related, or operational.

| Area | Metric | Healthy Signal | Report Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Attendance | Same-period year-over-year growth | Use the church's goal of 20%, but compare only equivalent periods | Show current period vs same weeks/months last year, not full prior year |
| Attendance | Momentum | 4-week and 12-week rolling average | Catch trend changes before monthly totals hide them |
| Events | Event lift | Event attendance vs normal baseline for same campus and season | Separate true growth from one-week event spikes |
| Events | Falloff | 1-week, 2-week, and 4-week retention after major Sundays | Show whether momentum lasted after a big push |
| First-time guests | FTG as percent of attendance | Internal target from focus412: 2-4% | Flag campuses with a closed front door or unusually strong invite culture |
| Guest retention | Return within 30/60/90 days | Needs people-level visit history | This is a higher-value metric than raw FTG count |
| Kids | Kids as percent of attendance | Internal target: 20-25%; Unstuck also references 20% as a benchmark | Indicates young-family reach and family ministry health |
| Volunteers | Volunteer roster as percent of attendance | Internal target: 40% | Use roster data when Planning Center/team data is available |
| Volunteers | Weekly volunteers per 100 attendance | Campus trend and service-time coverage | Current CSVs can support this immediately |
| Groups | Active groups as percent of attendance | Internal target: 6-10% group count to attendance | Needs group inventory, not just Growth Track data |
| Groups | Group participation | Internal target: 60%; national benchmarks often sit closer to 40-50% | Strong predictor of retention and discipleship depth |
| Growth Track | Completion rate | Internal target: 75% of signups complete all tracks | Requires signup and completion cohorts |
| Growth Track to Serving | Completed Growth Track attendees who sign up to serve | Internal target: 80% | Measures whether the pathway is producing contributors |
| Baptism | Baptisms as follow-through from salvations | Cohort-based conversion rate | Do not divide same-week totals blindly because baptism often lags |
| Leadership | Volunteer leader span of care | Unstuck notes 1 leader per 10 attenders as a benchmark and 1:5 as best-in-class | Measures ministry capacity without adding payroll |
| Staff Change | Transition-adjusted trend | Compare pre/post 8-12 weeks around CP/staff changes | Prevents misreading a leadership transition as only attendance volatility |

## Lessons From Large Church Models

### Church of the Highlands

Highlands uses a clear next-step pathway: Know God, Find Freedom, Discover Purpose, Make a Difference. Public pages connect Growth Track, Dream Team, baptism, and small groups into one integrated progression. The Growth Track is a recurring three-step process at every campus, and Dream Team is presented as the serving pathway after Growth Track.

Implication for reports: measure next-step flow, not isolated ministries. A strong campus report should show whether people are moving from Sunday attendance into Growth Track, groups, and serving.

### Life.Church

Life.Church Open Network emphasizes that attendance alone does not prove engagement. Their writing pushes leaders to ask whether people are taking next steps, serving, joining groups, and moving from spectators into mission. Their Church Metrics platform tracks categories like attendance, salvations, giving, volunteers, baptisms, and custom categories across service times and campuses.

Implication for reports: add a "spectator-to-participant" section. The highest-value analysis is not just "attendance is up"; it is "attendance is up, but volunteer engagement, group connection, or next-step conversion is/is not keeping pace."

### Elevation Church

Elevation publicly frames involvement around the Four G's: Gifts, Growth, Groups, and Giving. Public reporting discussed by MinistryWatch around Elevation's 2024 annual report shows why ratio analysis matters at scale: attendance, decisions, baptisms, volunteers, groups, outreach, and media reach can all be large, but executive analysis still needs to ask whether those categories are converting into durable engagement.

Implication for reports: separate reach from formation. A campus can have strong salvation, event, or online reach while still needing a stronger pathway into baptism, groups, serving, and recurring engagement.

## Report Sections To Build Next

1. Executive Summary

One paragraph that names the strongest signal, the biggest risk, and the recommended leadership action.

2. Campus Health Scorecard

Score each selected campus against its own baseline in five categories: attendance momentum, first-time guest flow, kids/family health, volunteer capacity, and next-step conversion.

3. Seasonality And Event Adjustment

Show normal seasonal pattern, event lift, and post-event falloff. A July dip should not be treated the same as a March collapse unless the campus is underperforming its own seasonal baseline.

4. Pathway Funnel

Report the funnel as: attendance -> first-time guests -> return guests -> Growth Track -> groups -> serving -> baptism. Use cohort rates where possible; avoid dividing unrelated same-period totals when the populations are not the same.

5. Capacity And Leadership Notes

Annotate campus pastor changes, staff transitions, service-time changes, and volunteer coverage shifts. When leadership changes occur, show pre/post averages and recovery velocity.

6. Opportunities And Recommendations

Make recommendations specific to the selected campus and selected period. Each recommendation should include the observed metric, why it matters, and the next action.

## Data We Have Now

- Weekly/service attendance
- Volunteers or Dream Teamers
- First-time guests
- Salvations
- Baptisms
- Kids
- Growth Track
- Campus
- Service date
- Service time for legacy summary files
- Notes/event labels from the source sheets

## Data To Add For Stronger Executive Analysis

- Planning Center people and visit history for return-guest retention
- Growth Track signup, attendance, and completion status by person
- Volunteer roster, team assignment, active/inactive status, and serve frequency
- Small group count, group leaders, enrollment, and attendance
- Campus pastor/staff transition timeline
- Service capacity by time, room, and kids capacity
- Giving households and recurring giver conversion if leadership wants financial health included
- Marketing/event calendar and campaign dates
